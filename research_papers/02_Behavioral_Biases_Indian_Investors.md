# Behavioral Biases and Investment Decisions: Research Compendium

## Paper 1: Cognitive Biases Impact on Retail Mutual Fund Investors

### Paper Link
- **European Economic Letters:** https://doi.org/10.52783/eel.v14i1.1131

### Author
Mansi Chadha

### Publication
European Economic Letters (EEL), Vol. 14 No. 1 (2024)

### Methodology
- Structured questionnaires distributed to **1,012 Indian mutual fund investors**
- Across four financial hub states: **Delhi/NCR, Mumbai, Bangalore, Kolkata**
- Multiple regression analysis with CFA for instrument validity

### Key Cognitive Biases Examined
1. **Overconfidence** - Investors overestimate their knowledge and ability
2. **Over-reaction** - Disproportionate response to market news
3. **Herd Effect** - Following crowd rather than independent analysis
4. **Regret Aversion** - Avoiding decisions to prevent potential regret

### Key Finding
All four cognitive biases demonstrated **significant** correlation with investor decision-making.

## Paper 2: Financial Literacy and Behavioral Biases on Investment Decision

### Paper Link
- **PMC:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9549276/
- **Note:** This paper has been retracted (Aug 2024), but its framework is still useful

### Authors
Sun Weixiang, Md Qamruzzaman, Wang Rui, Rajnish Kler

### Methodology
- 450 Indian investors surveyed
- Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) analysis
- Data collection: Sep 2021 - Jan 2022

### Four Primary Bias Categories
1. **Heuristic Bias** (anchoring, overconfidence, representativeness) - Coefficient: 0.434
2. **Framing Effect** (mental accounting, endowment, regret aversion) - Coefficient: -0.450
3. **Cognitive Illusions** (conservatism, confirmation, hindsight bias) - Coefficient: -0.274
4. **Herd Mentality** (information processing, bandwagon, social groups) - Coefficient: -0.256

## Paper 3: Studying the Influence of Cognitive Biases on Investment Decision-Making

### Paper Link
- **ACR Journal:** https://acr-journal.com/article/studying-the-influence-of-cognitive-biases-on-the-investment-decision-making-of-retail-investors-in-india-1513/

### Key Finding
The two most powerful factors accounting for 17.1% of variance:
- **Anchoring bias** (beta = 0.289)
- **Overconfidence bias** (beta = 0.250)
